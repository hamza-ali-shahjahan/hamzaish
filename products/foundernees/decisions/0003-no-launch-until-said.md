# 0003 — Nothing goes live until the operator says so (standing constraint)

- **Date**: 2026-08-16
- **Status**: ACTIVE — operator instruction, standing until explicitly lifted by the operator.
- **Decision**: **Do not deploy Foundernees to foundernees.com — or to any public URL —
  and do not propose deploying it.** No Vercel project, no DNS change, no "shall I ship
  it?" offer at the end of a task. The domain stays parked until the operator says the
  word, unprompted.
- **Why**: The operator's instruction, given plainly: *"first we have to make it work."*
  A movement's front door is a one-shot impression — the 14 August clock is public and a
  half-finished launch spends credibility that cannot be re-earned. Repeatedly offering
  to ship also puts a decision the operator has already made back on their desk every
  turn, which is its own kind of noise.
- **What this does NOT block**: building, testing, polish, SEO, content, accessibility,
  performance, and running the whole thing locally. Everything short of making it
  reachable by the public is in scope and should proceed at full speed.
- **Alternatives considered**: none, and that is the honest record. This is an operator
  instruction, not a judgement between options — the only "alternative" was to keep
  offering to ship, which is the behaviour the instruction exists to stop.
- **What would prove it wrong**: nothing internal. This is not an inference from
  evidence — it is an instruction, and only the operator lifts it.
- **Revisit trigger**: the operator says so. Not a milestone, not a green test run, not
  a "the site looks ready" judgment.
